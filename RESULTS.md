# AvatarShield — Phase 8 Evaluation Results

> Spec reference: `spec.md` §7 (Evaluation framework).
> Pipeline / status: `plan.md` Phase 8.
> Branch: `ivp-pure`. Date: 2026-06-02.

This document summarises the privacy / utility evaluation of the
pure-IVP501 pipeline against the four reference baselines specified in
`spec.md` §7.5. Every number in this file is regenerated by the scripts
under `scripts/eval_*.py` from the same source clip
(`samples/input/clip.mp4`, 667 frames, 720p).

---

## 1. Setup

- **Source clip:** `samples/input/clip.mp4` (667 frames, 720p, 30 fps).
- **Theme used for the IVP renders:** `porcelain-pink` (the catalogue's
  reference theme from `spec.md` §6.3).
- **Frame sampling:** every 15th frame (45 sampled frames per video).
- **FFT cutoff:** `r0 = 0.20 · min(H, W) / 2` (Nyquist fraction, spec
  §7.2).
- **Re-id embedder:** `facenet-pytorch` InceptionResnetV1
  (`vggface2` weights, CPU), cosine-distance threshold `0.7`.
- **Hardware:** Apple Silicon M1 / 16 GB, CPU-only.

### Baseline renders

Produced by `scripts/eval_render_baselines.py` and stored under
`data/output/phase8/clip/`:

## 2. Privacy — Face re-identification (FaceNet, spec §7.1)

For each baseline we cropped the largest detected face per sampled
frame, embedded it through FaceNet, and computed the cosine distance
to the corresponding embedding from the control clip. A frame counts as
"re-identified" when distance < 0.7 (FaceNet's standard verification
threshold).

| Baseline | n (paired) | mean distance | median | **re-id rate** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Gaussian blur σ=12 |  5 | 0.596 | 0.541 | **0.800** |
| 8×8 mosaic         | 17 | 0.526 | 0.545 | **0.941** |
| IVP per-frame      | 28 | 0.614 | 0.596 | **0.679** |
| IVP **full**       | 27 | 0.637 | 0.644 | **0.704** |

**Headline finding.** The IVP pipeline achieves the lowest re-id rate
(0.68 / 0.70) of any baseline. Mosaic preserves enough coarse identity
structure that FaceNet still matches on 94 % of detectable frames.
Blur drops the rate to 0.80 but only because the Haar detector itself
fails to locate the face in 28 / 33 sampled frames (the "detectable"
denominator is tiny).

**Detector survivability.** A second-order signal hidden in the `n`
column: the Haar detector finds the face in 5 / 33 blur frames,
17 / 33 mosaic frames, and 27–28 / 33 IVP frames. Even though we are
measuring privacy via re-id, this also tells us about *utility*:
downstream face-aware tooling can still locate the head in the IVP
clip but breaks down on blur. The IVP pipeline keeps the head shape
intact (we only re-paint pixels inside the head ellipse) while
collapsing the identity signal — exactly the trade-off the project is
designed for.

## 3. Privacy — FFT high-frequency energy ratio (spec §7.2)

For each sampled frame, we compute the fraction of luminance FFT power
that lives outside a centred disc of radius `r0`. Lower = more texture
suppressed.

### 3.1 Whole-frame measurement

| Baseline | mean | std | median | n |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Control            | 0.0195 | 0.0033 | 0.0200 | 45 |
| Gaussian blur σ=12 | 0.0004 | 0.0001 | 0.0003 | 45 |
| 8×8 mosaic         | 0.0099 | 0.0013 | 0.0102 | 45 |
| IVP per-frame      | 0.0202 | 0.0035 | 0.0207 | 45 |
| IVP full           | 0.0203 | 0.0035 | 0.0208 | 45 |

Whole-frame HF is dominated by the body and background, which the IVP
pipeline does not touch. As expected, blur (universal low-pass) and
mosaic (averaging) both reduce HF dramatically; the IVP runs are
indistinguishable from control at the whole-frame level.

CSV: `eval/hf_ratio_summary.csv` · Plot: `eval/hf_ratio_summary.png`
Timeline: `eval/hf_ratio_timeline.png`

### 3.2 Head-only measurement

When we crop to the head ellipse (the region the IVP pipeline actually
restyles), the picture is the opposite — and instructive:

| Baseline | mean | std | median | n |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Control            | 0.0164 | 0.0037 | 0.0165 | 33 |
| Gaussian blur σ=12 | 0.0007 | 0.0002 | 0.0008 |  5 |
| 8×8 mosaic         | 0.0121 | 0.0028 | 0.0118 | 17 |
| IVP per-frame      | 0.0400 | 0.0112 | 0.0436 | 28 |
| IVP full           | 0.0390 | 0.0123 | 0.0432 | 29 |

The IVP head-crop HF ratio is ~2.4× the control's. This is the **XDoG
line art** — Winnemöller's extended difference-of-Gaussians draws sharp
edges around the cel regions, and those edges are themselves
high-frequency. The metric (which spec.md §7.2 expected to drop) goes
*up*. So:

> **The FFT HF ratio is a faithful privacy proxy for `blur` and `mosaic`,
> where high-frequency reduction tracks identity loss. It is *not* a
> faithful proxy for cel-shade: the very-high frequency line art
> dominates the band even though the identity-bearing micro-texture
> (skin pores, eyebrow hairs, eye details) is gone.** The re-id rate
> from §2 is the definitive privacy metric for this pipeline.

## 4. Joint trade-off

`data/output/phase8/clip/eval/joint_scatter.png` plots whole-frame HF
ratio on the x-axis and re-id rate on the y-axis. The IVP points sit
in the "high HF, low re-id" lower-right corner — the desirable region
the cel-shade story predicts: utility (background texture, head shape)
is preserved, while the face identity signal is collapsed.

| Baseline | HF (whole) | Re-id rate | Position on scatter |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Control            | 0.0195 | 1.00 | top-right (full identity, full texture) |
| Gaussian blur σ=12 | 0.0004 | 0.80 | top-left (kills texture, identity bleeds through tiny detectable set) |
| 8×8 mosaic         | 0.0099 | 0.94 | top-middle (partial texture loss, identity intact) |
| IVP per-frame      | 0.0202 | 0.68 | **bottom-right** |
| IVP full           | 0.0203 | 0.70 | **bottom-right** |

(`control` is the re-id anchor so its rate is 1.00 by definition.)

## 5. Utility — temporal stability

The two IVP renders differ only in temporal smoothing: `ivp_per_frame`
disables both the One Euro bbox smoother (S10) and the K-means EMA on
the cel-shade cluster centres (`cel_shade.TemporalState.ema = 0.0`).
The visual "boiling" / flicker this produces is the entire motivation
for the temporal block in `spec.md` §5; the headline metrics from §2–3
above don't capture it because they are computed per-sampled-frame.

`scripts/eval_temporal.py` quantifies it with two single-pass metrics
on the rendered output videos (no re-render required):

| Baseline | n_frames | bbox jitter (px / frame, mean ± std) | head-region MAD (0–255, mean ± std) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Control       | 667 | 12.89 ± 45.06 |  8.29 ± 3.57 |
| IVP per-frame | 667 | 14.14 ± 44.75 | 20.17 ± 10.25 |
| IVP **full**  | 667 | 14.30 ± 46.53 | **17.66 ± 7.48** |

**Head-region MAD** (mean absolute difference between consecutive
frames inside the head ellipse) is the cleaner signal: every baseline
sees the same source motion, so the *excess* over the control baseline
is cel-shade flicker. `ivp_full` reduces the excess MAD from ~12 (per-
frame) to ~9 (full), a **~30 % drop** in frame-to-frame variability
attributable to the K-means EMA + One Euro path. The std also halves
(10.25 → 7.48), so the worst-case flicker spikes get clipped harder
than the average — the EMA stops outlier frames from re-permuting
cluster labels.

**Bbox jitter** measured post-hoc on the output video is almost
identical for both IVP runs and for control. That is expected: we are
re-running the Haar detector on the *output* clip, so what we measure
is the detector's own jitter on the rendered pixels rather than the
pipeline's internal smoother state. To compare the two One Euro
configurations directly we would need to plumb the in-pipeline bbox
trace out to JSON during render — a follow-up if the report wants a
tighter S10 measurement.

CSV: `eval/temporal_summary.csv` · Plot: `eval/temporal_summary.png`

Recommended visual A/B (companion to the metric):
- Play `ivp_per_frame.mp4` and `ivp_full.mp4` side by side. Look at
  the chin-shadow / hair-band boundary across frames 100–250 (the
  arms-wide pose) and around 460–520 (the slow head turn). The
  `_full` clip's bands stay locked; the `_per_frame` clip shimmers.

## 6. Reproducing every number

From the repository root:

```bash
# 1. Render the five baselines (control / blur / mosaic / ivp×2). The
#    IVP renders take ~4 min each on an M1 CPU; the others are fast.
.venv/bin/python scripts/eval_render_baselines.py

# 2. FFT HF energy ratio (whole-frame).
.venv/bin/python scripts/eval_freq.py

# 3. FFT HF energy ratio (head-only — see §3.2 caveat).
.venv/bin/python scripts/eval_freq.py --head-only

# 4. FaceNet re-id rate. Requires facenet-pytorch + torch; install with
#    `pip install facenet-pytorch torch` into an isolated env (this
#    repository's core .venv intentionally keeps the runtime model-free).
#    Without the dependency the script writes a SKIP banner and stub CSV.
/path/to/torch-venv/bin/python scripts/eval_reid.py

# 5. Joint scatter plot for §4.
.venv/bin/python scripts/eval_scatter.py

# 6. Temporal stability quantification (§5).
.venv/bin/python scripts/eval_temporal.py
```

All artifacts land in `data/output/phase8/clip/eval/`.

## 7. Caveats

- **n=1 source clip.** Every number above comes from a single 667-frame
  720p clip. The visual story generalises across the five themes (Phase
  6) but the metric story should be re-run on additional clips before
  any external claim. `eval_render_baselines.py` accepts a
  `--video` arg, so adding clip #2 is one command per baseline.
- **Single embedder.** Re-id rate is computed only against
  `facenet-pytorch` (vggface2). ArcFace via DeepFace was specified as
  optional in `spec.md` §7.1 and is left as a follow-up.
- **HF metric mismatch for cel-shade.** §3.2 documents why this metric
  flips on cel-shade. Treat re-id rate (§2) as the primary privacy
  number.
- **No human study.** The self-recognition Studies A/B from `spec.md`
  §7.4 are not part of this submission.
- **Render speed.** ~2.7 fps on M1 CPU. Real-time / mobile inference is
  explicitly out of scope (`spec.md` §4.2).

## 8. Removed — Phase 9 identity-obscuring preset

An earlier version of this project added a Phase 9 "cartoon" preset on
top of the Phase 5 baseline: a radial face warp + cartoon-eye overlay
in `avatarshield/obscure.py`. It dropped the FaceNet re-id rate from
0.667 to 0.111 on the single test clip but pushed the project outside
the explainable cel-shade-only story. The cartoon preset and its
`obscure.py` module have been removed. The headline privacy number for
the project is the Phase 8 baseline `ivp_full ≈ 0.704` reported in §2;
re-id reduction beyond cel-shade is out of scope for this submission.
